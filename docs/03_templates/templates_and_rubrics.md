# Templates & Rubrics

**Status:** Canonical
**Version:** 1.0
**Project:** Invest Smarter with AI
**Domain Authority:** Canonical forms, checklist schemas, deliverable fields, scoring rubrics, and completion thresholds.

Curriculum specifies which artifacts students produce and teaches their use. [Investment Methodology](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md) governs investment meaning; [Research Standards](../05_research/data_and_research_standards.md) governs evidence, citations, freshness, and verification.

Use Methodology's [research dispositions and thesis statuses](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md#stage-8--decide) and [portfolio actions](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md#14-buy--hold--add--trim--sell). WATCH is a research disposition, not a thesis status. BUY CANDIDATE is a research conclusion; BUY is a portfolio action after entry, sizing, and risk are accepted.

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the canonical templates students use throughout the program.

The templates are designed to:

- reduce analysis friction;
- create consistency;
- improve decision quality;
- prevent unnecessary research;
- make AI-assisted work auditable;
- help students build a reusable personal investment operating system.

The goal is not to produce long reports.

The goal is to capture the minimum information required to make a better decision.

---

# 2. Core Design Principle

Every template should answer:

1. What am I trying to decide?
2. What evidence matters?
3. What remains uncertain?
4. What could make me wrong?
5. What action follows?

The student should not fill a template simply because a field exists.

If a field does not materially improve the decision, keep it concise.

---

# 3. Rubric Philosophy

Students are evaluated on **process quality**, not future market outcome.

A strong analysis can lead to a losing investment.

A weak analysis can lead to a winning investment.

The rubric therefore prioritizes:

- clarity;
- evidence;
- relevance;
- risk awareness;
- uncertainty;
- decision logic;
- source quality;
- AI verification.

---

# 4. Standard Rating Scale

Use the following four-level scale.

## 4 — Strong

- clear;
- evidence-based;
- decision-relevant;
- risks are explicit;
- uncertainty is acknowledged;
- conclusion follows from analysis;
- AI output is verified.

## 3 — Sufficient

- mostly clear;
- enough evidence to support the decision;
- some gaps remain;
- major risks are identified;
- conclusion is reasonable.

## 2 — Weak

- incomplete;
- too descriptive;
- insufficient evidence;
- important risks missing;
- too much reliance on assumptions or AI output;
- decision logic is weak.

## 1 — Not Ready

- major sections missing;
- unsupported conclusions;
- unclear decision;
- material facts unverified;
- risk ignored;
- analysis should not be used for a real investment decision.

---

# 5. Universal Evaluation Rubric

Use this rubric across all major deliverables.

| Dimension | 4 — Strong | 3 — Sufficient | 2 — Weak | 1 — Not Ready |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Decision and reasoning are immediately understandable | Mostly clear | Ambiguous in important areas | Cannot explain the decision |
| Evidence | Material claims supported by reliable sources | Most key claims supported | Important claims weakly supported | Claims largely unsupported |
| Relevance | Focuses on decision-driving information | Mostly relevant | Too much noise or missing priorities | Analysis is unfocused |
| Risk | Main downside and invalidation clearly identified | Major risks covered | Risks generic or incomplete | Risk materially ignored |
| Uncertainty | Facts, estimates, and unknowns clearly separated | Some uncertainty acknowledged | Assumptions insufficiently labeled | False certainty |
| Decision Logic | Conclusion follows from evidence | Reasonable conclusion | Conclusion partially disconnected | Decision appears arbitrary |
| AI Verification | AI used efficiently and key facts verified | AI mostly verified | Heavy AI dependence | AI output treated as truth |

A score of **3 or above in every material dimension** is the default threshold for a deliverable to be considered complete.

# 19. AI Red-Team Prompt

Use after a thesis exists.

> Assume my thesis is wrong.
>
> Find the strongest evidence against it.
>
> Focus on:
> - structural deterioration;
> - competitive threats;
> - financial weakness;
> - valuation risk;
> - customer/supplier risk;
> - industry change;
> - reasons the market may be correct.
>
> Separate confirmed evidence from hypotheses.
>
> Do not repeat my bull case.

## 000 — Investing Foundations

Student can:

- explain what they are buying;
- distinguish saving/investing/speculating;
- define horizon and liquidity;
- distinguish risk capacity from tolerance;
- protect money that should not be invested.

## 201 — Understand Any Business

Student can:

- explain the business simply;
- map industry and supply chain;
- identify economics;
- identify 3–5 relevant KPIs;
- identify competitive advantage and vulnerabilities;
- know when to stop or deepen research.

## 301 — What Is It Worth?

Student can:

- use appropriate valuation metrics;
- explain market expectations;
- construct reasonable scenarios;
- identify potential value traps;
- use ranges rather than false precision.

---

## 401 — Spot the Opportunity

Student can:

- explain the dislocation;
- distinguish temporary vs structural;
- assess catalysts when useful, accept a justified no-catalyst conclusion, and identify risk;
- define entry zone and invalidation;
- avoid buying merely because price fell.

---

## 501 — Manage Your Portfolio

Student can:

- monitor thesis rather than price alone;
- identify concentration;
- prioritize research;
- classify BUY/HOLD/ADD/TRIM/SELL;
- know when no action is required.

# 24. Instructor Review Checklist

Before approving a deliverable, ask:

- Is the decision clear?
- Are primary sources used where appropriate?
- Are important claims dated?
- Are facts separated from assumptions?
- Is the student focusing on the key drivers?
- Are major risks explicit?
- Is invalidation defined?
- Has AI output been verified?
- Is the work proportionate to the decision?
- Could the student explain the thesis without reading the template?

---

# 25. Anti-Overanalysis Rule

Templates are decision tools, not bureaucratic forms.

Do not require every field to be filled in every situation. Apply the [Methodology research escalation rule](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md#23-research-escalation-rule) rather than a separate investment gate.

The student should spend more time only when:

- the opportunity is material;
- uncertainty can be reduced;
- the expected payoff justifies the research.

If the decision is already clearly PASS, stop.

---

# 26. Templates North Star

The student should finish the program with a reusable system that allows them to move from:

**Idea → Research → Understanding → Valuation → Opportunity → Decision → Monitoring**

without rebuilding the process from scratch every time.
