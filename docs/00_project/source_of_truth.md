# 1. Project Vision

## 1.1 What We Are Building

A practical investment education program for busy professionals, executives, specialists, and entrepreneurs who want to build wealth with judgment but do not have the time or desire to become full-time analysts or traders.

The program teaches students to:

- understand how markets and investment instruments work;
- build a coherent personal investment system;
- invest through ETFs and individual stocks;
- understand a business quickly;
- learn unfamiliar industries and supply chains efficiently;
- interpret the financial metrics that actually matter;
- estimate reasonable valuation ranges;
- identify undervaluation and market dislocations;
- recognize catalysts and attractive entry points;
- decide when to buy, hold, add, trim, or sell;
- manage a portfolio without watching markets all day;
- use AI to compress research time while preserving human judgment.

---

## 1.2 Core Promise

**Think like an investor without becoming a full-time analyst.**

The student should learn to make informed medium- to long-term investment decisions using a simple, repeatable, AI-augmented process.

---

## 1.3 Positioning

Working program name:

# Invest Smarter with AI

Working value proposition:

> Learn to understand businesses, spot opportunities, value investments, and manage your portfolio efficiently — without spending hours analyzing the market.

Internal principle:

> **AI does the heavy lifting. The investor makes the decision.**

# 2. Target Audience

## 2.1 Primary Student

The primary student is a:

- professional;
- executive;
- specialist;
- entrepreneur;
- business owner;

who:

- has income and some investable capital;
- wants to build wealth over the medium to long term;
- has limited time for research;
- does not want to analyze markets for hours every day;
- does not want to become a professional equity analyst;
- wants to understand what they own and why;
- wants to detect attractive opportunities;
- wants to identify undervalued companies and temporary dislocations;
- wants to understand industries, business models, and supply chains quickly;
- wants enough financial knowledge to make better decisions;
- wants to use AI as a research accelerator.

---

## 2.2 Time Constraint

Time scarcity is a core product constraint.

The program should help the student:

- avoid unnecessary research;
- prioritize what matters;
- automate low-value observation;
- use alerts;
- use AI to summarize and structure information;
- escalate analysis only when an opportunity deserves it.

A typical student may dedicate:

- limited monthly time to portfolio maintenance;
- 30–60 minutes to understand a promising company;
- 1–2 additional hours when a potentially exceptional opportunity deserves deeper work.

---

## 2.3 What the Student Is Not Trying to Become

The core program is not designed to produce:

- professional equity analysts;
- day traders;
- quantitative traders;
- derivatives specialists;
- accountants;
- full-time market operators;
- DCF modelers for its own sake.

Advanced content should not be created simply for academic completeness.

Only create additional courses when:

- students explicitly ask for them;
- repeated demand is observed;
- a clear educational need emerges from actual usage.

# 3. Pedagogical Philosophy

## 3.1 Learn → Understand → Decide → Execute

Every concept should improve a real investment decision.

A student does not complete a course because they watched all the slides.

They complete it when they can execute the required skill.

---

## 3.2 Decision Quality per Unit of Time

The program should optimize for:

> **better decisions with less wasted research.**

Students should not learn every available metric.

They should learn the metrics that materially change decisions.

---

## 3.3 Depth Rule

Every concept belongs to one of three levels.

### Must Know
Required for reasonable decision-making.

### Useful to Know
Improves context or judgment.

### Only When Needed
Introduced only when the investment or industry requires it.

Do not create permanent advanced modules merely because a topic exists.

---

## 3.4 Research Escalation

Students should learn to increase research depth only when justified.

### Rapid Screen
Is this worth investigating?

### Business Understanding
How does the business and industry work?

### Opportunity Analysis
Is the valuation or dislocation attractive?

### Monitoring
Has anything material changed?

Not every company deserves deep research.

---

# 4. AI Is Embedded, Not a Separate Course

AI should not be added to course titles as “+ AI”.

AI is part of the operating method across the entire program.

Each course should include:

**Concept → Framework → AI Workflow → Verification → Human Decision**

---

## 4.1 Appropriate AI Uses

AI can help:

- explain concepts;
- summarize filings;
- extract financial data;
- compare brokers;
- compare ETFs;
- map industries;
- map supply chains;
- analyze earnings;
- compare competitors;
- identify KPIs;
- structure valuation scenarios;
- investigate dislocations;
- identify catalysts;
- challenge investment theses;
- update investment memos;
- prioritize portfolio review.

---

## 4.2 AI Is Not the Decision Maker

Students should not begin with:

> Should I buy this stock?

They should learn to ask:

- How does this business make money?
- What drives its economics?
- How does the industry work?
- Where is the company in the value chain?
- What expectations are embedded in the price?
- Why did the stock move?
- Is the problem temporary or structural?
- What evidence would invalidate my thesis?
- What information am I missing?
- What must I verify in a primary source?

The final decision remains human.

---

## 4.3 AI Verification

Material investment conclusions should require:

- date checks;
- primary-source verification;
- source citation;
- clear separation of fact and inference;
- explicit uncertainty;
- critical-number verification;
- contradiction checks.

# 201 — Understand Any Business

**Promise:** Learn how a company makes money, how its industry works, and where it sits in the value chain.

This is a core differentiator of the program.

The student should be able to build a first useful view of an unfamiliar business in approximately 30–60 minutes.

## Core Framework

1. Business model
2. Industry
3. Value chain / supply chain
4. Economics
5. Competitive position
6. Key industry KPIs
7. Risks

## Business Model

Understand:

- product;
- customer;
- revenue model;
- segments;
- geographies;
- pricing;
- volume;
- recurrence.

## Industry

Understand:

- major players;
- market structure;
- cyclicality;
- barriers;
- substitutes;
- regulation.

## Supply Chain

Map:

**Inputs → Suppliers → Production → Distribution → Customer**

Identify:

- bottlenecks;
- critical suppliers;
- customer concentration;
- geographic risk;
- commodity exposure;
- pricing power.

## Financial Minimum

Understand the metrics that explain the business:

- revenue;
- gross margin;
- operating margin;
- EPS;
- FCF;
- CapEx;
- debt;
- share count;
- ROIC when useful.

## Sector-Specific KPIs

Teach students to identify the 3–5 metrics that matter for each industry.

Examples:

- SaaS;
- semiconductors;
- retail;
- banks;
- REITs;
- industrials.

## AI Role

Use AI to:

- summarize filings;
- teach unfamiliar industries;
- map value chains;
- identify major competitors;
- identify the few KPIs that matter;
- surface dependencies and risks.

## Deliverable

**Rapid Business Map**

Decision:

- PASS;
- WATCHLIST;
- DEEPER RESEARCH.

# 301 — What Is It Worth?

**Promise:** Understand valuation, expectations, and when price disconnects from value.

## Core Skills

- price vs value;
- valuation drivers;
- P/E;
- Forward P/E;
- EV/EBITDA;
- Price / FCF;
- FCF yield;
- historical valuation;
- peer valuation;
- expectations;
- scenario analysis;
- fair-value ranges;
- margin of safety;
- value traps.

## Core Principle

Cheap ≠ undervalued.

Students should learn to ask:

> What does the current price require the company to achieve?

## Scenario Framework

Use:

- Bear;
- Base;
- Bull.

Avoid false precision.

## AI Role

Use AI to:

- normalize financial tables;
- compare valuation history;
- compare peers;
- structure scenarios;
- identify embedded expectations.

## Deliverable

**Valuation & Expectations Card**

Includes:

- current valuation;
- historical context;
- peer context;
- market expectations;
- bear / base / bull;
- reasonable valuation range;
- main valuation risk.

# 401 — Spot the Opportunity

**Promise:** Find dislocations, catalysts, and attractive entry points without becoming a full-time trader.

## Core Framework

**Quality + Mispricing + Catalyst + Setup**

## Dislocation

Ask:

> Did the price move more than the underlying value?

## Temporary vs Structural

Students must distinguish:

- temporary operational or cyclical issues;
- permanent deterioration.

## Catalyst

Potential examples:

- margin normalization;
- inventory recovery;
- cycle recovery;
- new product;
- new customer;
- regulatory approval;
- deleveraging;
- buyback;
- restructuring.

## Market Context

Use only what improves the decision:

- broad market trend;
- sector trend;
- industry cycle;
- earnings calendar;
- relevant macro context.

## Technical Minimum

Use:

- trend;
- support / resistance;
- volume;
- 50DMA;
- 200DMA;
- RSI.

Technical analysis supports timing. It does not replace the thesis.

## Entry

Define:

- ideal zone;
- acceptable zone;
- no-chase level;
- scaling logic;
- invalidation;
- position size.

## AI Role

Use AI to:

- investigate why a stock moved;
- separate facts from narrative;
- compare peers;
- determine whether weakness is company-specific or sector-wide;
- build catalyst maps;
- red-team the opportunity.

## Deliverable

**Opportunity Card**

Decision:

- BUY CANDIDATE;
- WATCH;
- PASS.

# 501 — Manage Your Portfolio

**Promise:** Know when to buy, hold, add, trim, or sell without watching the market all day.

## Core Skills

- portfolio as a system;
- Core / Satellite management;
- concentration;
- position limits;
- sector limits;
- thesis-based monitoring;
- earnings review;
- watchlists;
- alerts;
- rebalancing;
- performance;
- opportunity cost.

## Decision States

Every position should fit one state:

- BUY;
- HOLD;
- ADD;
- TRIM;
- SELL.

## Sell Framework

Potential reasons to sell:

- thesis broken;
- moat deteriorates;
- financial quality deteriorates;
- structural industry change;
- valuation becomes extreme;
- opportunity cost becomes too high;
- position becomes too large.

Do not sell only because price moved.

## Monthly Routine

Target approximately one hour when no exceptional event requires deeper work.

### Portfolio Health
- allocation;
- cash;
- concentration.

### Positions
- thesis;
- earnings;
- risks.

### Watchlist
- dislocations;
- catalysts;
- alerts.

### Decisions
- buy;
- hold;
- add;
- trim;
- sell;
- rebalance;
- no action.

## AI Role

Use AI to:

- update an existing thesis after earnings;
- compare results with prior assumptions;
- identify material changes;
- prioritize portfolio review;
- challenge sell decisions;
- prioritize watchlist research.

## Deliverable

**Portfolio Operating System**

# 9. Content Design Rules

## One Main Idea per Slide

Avoid turning slides into textbook pages.

## Prefer Visual Frameworks

Use:

- diagrams;
- decision trees;
- checklists;
- scorecards;
- comparisons;
- charts;
- business maps;
- supply-chain maps;
- real cases.

## Fewer Metrics, Better Decisions

Every metric should answer:

> What decision becomes better because I know this?

## Real Financial Language

Use standard financial terminology, initially explained clearly.

## No False Precision

Use ranges and scenarios for:

- fair value;
- forecasts;
- probabilities;
- risk/reward.

## Risk First

Every investment example should consider:

- what can go wrong;
- downside;
- horizon;
- concentration;
- thesis invalidation.

# 15. Content Addition Rule

Before adding any topic, ask:

1. Does it improve a real investment decision?
2. Does the target student need it now?
3. Does it reduce material risk?
4. Can it be taught more simply?
5. Can AI reduce the manual work?
6. What action does it enable?
7. What existing content can be removed or compressed?
8. Is there demonstrated demand?

If the answer is unclear, the topic probably does not belong in the core program.

# 16. Program North Star

At the end of the program, the student should not think:

> I know hundreds of financial metrics.

They should think:

> **I know what I own, I can understand a business quickly, I can judge whether price and value are disconnected, I know what could make me wrong, and I have a system I can manage without making investing my full-time job.**

That outcome should govern every curriculum, product, content, and design decision.
