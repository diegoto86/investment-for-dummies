# Legal, Risk & Compliance Guardrails

**Status:** Canonical Internal Draft  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Project:** Invest Smarter with AI  
**Domain Authority:** Educational boundaries, risk disclosures, examples, personalization limits, conflicts, and pre-launch compliance requirements.

> This document is an internal product guardrail, not legal advice.
>
> Before commercial launch, jurisdiction-specific review by qualified legal/compliance professionals is required.

---

# 1. Purpose

The program teaches investment decision-making.

It must not create a false impression that:

- investing is safe;
- returns are guaranteed;
- losses can be eliminated;
- AI can reliably predict markets;
- educational examples are personalized financial advice.

The product should preserve student agency and make risk visible.

---

# 2. Educational Positioning

Default positioning:

**Investment education and decision-framework training.**

The product should teach:

- how instruments work;
- how to research;
- how to analyze;
- how to evaluate trade-offs;
- how to manage risk;
- how to structure a decision.

It should not automatically tell an individual:

> You personally should invest X euros in security Y.

unless the product is intentionally designed and legally authorized to provide such advice.

---

# 3. Personalized Advice Boundary

Educational personalization may include:

- explaining risk capacity;
- asking about objectives;
- demonstrating asset-allocation concepts;
- helping the student complete a self-assessment;
- showing how different choices affect risk.

Avoid turning educational workflows into an automated suitability recommendation engine unless legally reviewed and intentionally designed.

---

# 4. Student Deliverables

Deliverables such as:

- Investor Profile;
- Investment System;
- Opportunity Card;
- Portfolio Operating System;

should be framed as student-created educational work.

Canonical artifact fields and rubrics are defined in [Templates & Rubrics](../03_templates/templates_and_rubrics.md). Evidence verification follows [Research Standards](../05_research/data_and_research_standards.md). These forms do not establish legal suitability or authorize personalized advice.

The student remains responsible for:

- verifying information;
- determining suitability;
- deciding whether to transact;
- understanding tax/legal consequences.

---

# 5. No Guarantee Language

Avoid:

- safe investment;
- guaranteed return;
- guaranteed income;
- can't lose;
- low-risk profit;
- sure thing;
- protected upside;
- AI will find winners.

Prefer:

- lower/higher risk;
- historically less volatile;
- potential return;
- estimated range;
- scenario;
- uncertainty;
- possible downside.

---

# 6. Performance Claims

Do not imply that:

- historical returns will continue;
- a backtest guarantees real-world performance;
- a successful example proves a method always works;
- selected winners represent typical results.

Past performance must be presented as historical evidence, not future assurance.

---

# 7. Hypothetical Examples

Clearly label:

- hypothetical;
- simulated;
- paper trade;
- historical case study.

Do not blur hypothetical gains with actual track records.

---

# 8. Real Investment Examples

When using a real security:

- date the example;
- disclose that prices and facts can change;
- explain educational purpose;
- include material risks;
- avoid presenting the example as universal advice.

---

# 9. Conflicts of Interest

If the instructor, company, or content creator:

- owns the security;
- receives sponsorship;
- receives affiliate compensation;
- has a commercial relationship;

the conflict should be disclosed when relevant.

Educational credibility depends on transparency.

---

# 10. Broker and Product Comparisons

Broker or ETF comparisons should use disclosed criteria.

If affiliate relationships exist:

- disclose them;
- do not allow compensation to silently determine rankings;
- preserve objective comparison criteria.

---

# 11. AI Guardrails

AI should not be presented as:

- a licensed adviser;
- a guaranteed forecasting tool;
- a replacement for primary sources;
- an autonomous portfolio manager unless intentionally designed and legally authorized.

Students should be told to verify:

- prices;
- financial data;
- legal terms;
- tax information;
- regulatory information.

---

# 12. AI Output Labels

Where appropriate, distinguish:

- source-derived facts;
- estimates;
- AI inference;
- educational scenarios.

The product should not hide uncertainty behind authoritative wording.

---

# 13. Risk Disclosure Principles

Investment content should make relevant risks visible.

Depending on context:

- market risk;
- business risk;
- liquidity risk;
- currency risk;
- interest-rate risk;
- credit risk;
- concentration risk;
- leverage risk;
- regulatory risk;
- tax uncertainty.

Not every slide needs a legal disclaimer.

Risk should be integrated into the decision framework.

---

# 14. Leverage and Derivatives

Leveraged investing or trading strategies and derivatives strategies are outside the core program. Business operating leverage may still be taught as a normal business-analysis concept. Contextual education about derivatives remains permitted within the [Source of Truth scope](../00_project/source_of_truth.md#52-context-only-asset-classes). This does not authorize deeper leverage or derivatives curriculum.

If mentioned for context:

- explain asymmetric risk;
- explain possibility of rapid loss;
- avoid operational encouragement unless a dedicated, reviewed module is later created.

Do not add advanced products simply to make the curriculum appear sophisticated.

---

# 15. Crypto and Speculative Assets

When discussed:

- clearly explain volatility;
- explain custody/technology/regulatory risks;
- avoid treating price appreciation as evidence of intrinsic value;
- avoid presenting speculative assets as necessary components of a portfolio.

---

# 16. Tax Content

Tax rules are jurisdiction- and time-dependent.

When taxes are mentioned:

- date the information;
- identify jurisdiction;
- use authoritative sources;
- encourage professional verification for material decisions.

Do not hard-code tax assumptions into evergreen lessons without a maintenance plan.

---

# 17. Regulatory Content

Regulation changes.

For:

- broker protection;
- investor compensation;
- product eligibility;
- financial promotions;
- advisory status;

use current authoritative sources and date the information.

---

# 18. Suitability and Risk Capacity

The course may teach the difference between:

- risk tolerance;
- risk capacity.

Do not imply that a questionnaire automatically establishes legal suitability for a specific product unless legally reviewed.

---

# 19. Vulnerable Situations

Avoid encouraging market investment with:

- emergency funds;
- money needed for near-term obligations;
- borrowed money when risk is not explicitly understood;
- capital the student cannot afford to lose.

---

# 20. Educational Language

Prefer:

> Here is a framework to evaluate whether this fits your objectives.

over:

> You should buy this.

Prefer:

> This valuation appears attractive under these assumptions.

over:

> This stock will rise.

---

# 21. Marketing Guardrails

Marketing may promise:

- efficiency;
- structure;
- improved understanding;
- better research workflow;
- AI-assisted learning.

Marketing should not promise:

- specific returns;
- wealth guarantees;
- beating the market;
- risk-free investing;
- becoming rich quickly.

---

# 22. Program Name and Messaging

"Invest Smarter with AI" may communicate efficiency.

Avoid pairing it with claims such as:

- safe profits;
- guaranteed success;
- easy money;
- automatic winners.

A strong compliant message is:

> Think like an investor without becoming a full-time analyst.

---

# 23. Pre-Launch Compliance Checklist

Before commercialization, review:

- target jurisdictions;
- educational vs advisory boundary;
- website disclosures;
- terms and conditions;
- privacy;
- data handling;
- payment/refund terms;
- financial-promotion rules;
- affiliate disclosures;
- instructor conflicts;
- student examples/testimonials;
- performance claims;
- AI disclosures;
- broker/product references;
- tax content.

---

# 24. Legal Review Trigger

Obtain professional legal/compliance review before adding features such as:

- personalized security recommendations;
- automated portfolio recommendations;
- trade execution;
- broker integration;
- copy trading;
- model portfolios presented as personalized;
- paid signals;
- performance marketing;
- portfolio management.

---

# 25. Maintenance

Compliance documentation should be revisited when:

- product geography changes;
- product features change;
- monetization changes;
- personalization increases;
- AI capabilities change;
- broker/trading integrations are added;
- regulations materially change.

---

# 26. Compliance North Star

The product should empower students to make their own informed decisions while being transparent about:

- uncertainty;
- risk;
- evidence;
- conflicts;
- the educational nature of the program.
