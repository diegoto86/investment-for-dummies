# 501 — Manage Your Portfolio

This course applies the audience, scope, and six-course architecture in the [Source of Truth](../00_project/source_of_truth.md). Analytical principles follow [Investment Methodology](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md); sourcing and verification follow [Research Standards](../05_research/data_and_research_standards.md). [Templates & Rubrics](../03_templates/templates_and_rubrics.md) owns form schemas and completion thresholds.

## Course promise

**Know when to buy, hold, add, trim or sell without watching the market all day.**

This course turns individual investments into a coherent portfolio operating system.

The target student has a job or business.

Therefore the portfolio must be designed for:

- low operational friction;
- scheduled review;
- event-driven monitoring;
- clear rules;
- AI-assisted information compression.

The goal is not daily activity.

The goal is high-quality decisions when action is actually required.

---

# 1. What the student should be able to do

By the end of the course, the student should be able to:

- manage Core and Satellite exposure;
- monitor concentration;
- set position and sector limits;
- distinguish thesis risk from price volatility;
- decide when to add, hold, trim or sell;
- review earnings efficiently;
- maintain a watchlist;
- run a monthly portfolio review;
- use AI to update an existing thesis;
- automate alerts and information intake;
- avoid unnecessary portfolio activity.

---

# 2. Lesson 1 — Portfolio as a system

A portfolio is not a list of stocks.

It is a system of:

- objectives;
- exposures;
- risks;
- theses;
- cash;
- rules;
- decisions.

## Key question

> If several positions fail at the same time, what common factor could be responsible?

---

# 3. Lesson 2 — Core / Satellite management

## Core

Designed to require limited intervention.

Possible review cadence:

- quarterly;
- semiannual;
- annual allocation review.

## Satellite

Requires thesis-specific monitoring.

Possible review triggers:

- earnings;
- guidance change;
- material news;
- valuation threshold;
- catalyst.

---

# 4. Lesson 3 — Concentration

Monitor:

- position size;
- sector exposure;
- geography;
- factor exposure;
- correlated business models.

## Important

Concentration may be intentional.

The danger is unintended concentration.

---

# 5. Lesson 4 — Position limits

Define before emotions become involved.

Examples of policy questions:

- maximum starting position;
- maximum full position;
- maximum sector exposure;
- when trimming is required;
- when a position may exceed its normal limit.

The course should teach principles, not universal percentages.

---

# 6. Lesson 5 — Buy / Hold / Add / Trim / Sell

Use the [Methodology portfolio actions](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md#14-buy--hold--add--trim--sell). The explanations below are teaching summaries of those canonical semantics.

## BUY
Portfolio action after entry, sizing, and risk considerations are accepted. BUY CANDIDATE remains a research conclusion until then.

## HOLD
Thesis intact, valuation acceptable, no better action required.

## ADD
Thesis intact or improving, expected return still attractive.

## TRIM
Position too large, valuation stretched or risk increased.

## SELL
Thesis broken, opportunity cost too high or valuation/extreme risk changes the decision.

---

# 7. Lesson 6 — When to sell

Potential reasons:

- thesis is broken;
- competitive advantage deteriorates;
- financial quality materially worsens;
- structural industry change;
- valuation becomes extreme;
- catalyst is realized and expected return collapses;
- a materially better risk-adjusted opportunity exists.

Excessive position size alone maps to TRIM. SELL is reserved for the existing Methodology sell conditions.

## Not sufficient by itself

- stock is down 10%;
- market had a bad week;
- negative headline without thesis impact;
- "I want to lock in profit."

---

# 8. Lesson 7 — Thesis-based monitoring

For every individual stock, maintain:

- original thesis;
- expected drivers;
- key KPIs;
- catalysts;
- risks;
- invalidation conditions.

Then update only what changed.

## Principle

Do not rebuild the company analysis every quarter.

**Update the thesis.**

---

# 9. Lesson 8 — Earnings review

After results, answer:

- revenue vs expectation;
- margin vs expectation;
- FCF;
- guidance;
- key KPIs;
- management commentary;
- capital allocation;
- thesis change.

## Decision

- IMPROVING;
- UNCHANGED;
- DETERIORATING;
- BROKEN.

These are thesis statuses under [Methodology](../02_methodology/investment_methodology.md#stage-8--decide), not research dispositions or portfolio actions.

---

# 10. Lesson 9 — Monthly Investment Routine

Use the [Source of Truth ordinary monthly review budget](../00_project/source_of_truth.md#22-time-constraint). The four-part allocation below is a teaching example for that budget, not a separate required form.

## 15 minutes — Portfolio health

Check:

- allocation;
- cash;
- concentration;
- sector exposure;
- major performance drivers.

## 15 minutes — Existing positions

Check:

- thesis status;
- earnings;
- major news;
- valuation changes.

## 15 minutes — Watchlist

Check:

- price alerts;
- dislocations;
- upcoming catalysts;
- new opportunities.

## 15 minutes — Decisions

Classify:

- buy;
- hold;
- add;
- trim;
- sell;
- rebalance;
- no action.

---

# 11. Lesson 10 — Rebalancing

Possible methods:

- calendar-based;
- threshold-based;
- thesis-based.

## Avoid

Rebalancing mechanically when the underlying economics have changed.

---

# 12. Lesson 11 — Performance

## Must know

- total return;
- CAGR;
- benchmark;
- max drawdown;
- contribution by position.

## More important

Process quality.

Ask:

- Was the thesis sound?
- Was sizing appropriate?
- Was the decision consistent with the process?
- Was the outcome driven by skill or luck?

---

# 13. Lesson 12 — Opportunity cost

Capital allocated to one position cannot be allocated elsewhere.

Ask:

> If I did not already own this position, would I buy it today at this price?

This helps detect emotional attachment.

---

# 14. Lesson 13 — Watchlists

Useful categories:

- Core candidates;
- Quality businesses;
- Valuation watch;
- Dislocations;
- Earnings watch;
- Sector themes.

## Rule

A watchlist is not a shopping list.

It is a prioritized research queue.

---

# 15. Lesson 14 — Alerts

Automate observation where possible.

Possible alerts:

- price;
- valuation threshold;
- earnings date;
- guidance;
- insider transaction;
- material news;
- technical level.

## Principle

> Do not stare at the market if an alert can tell you when something relevant happens.

---

# 16. AI workflow — Earnings update

Provide:

- previous investment memo;
- latest earnings release;
- transcript;
- guidance.

## Prompt

> Compare the new results with my existing investment thesis. Do not create a new thesis from scratch.
>
> Identify:
> - what improved;
> - what deteriorated;
> - what stayed unchanged;
> - guidance changes;
> - KPI changes;
> - new risks;
> - capital-allocation changes;
> - assumptions that are now invalid.
>
> Classify the thesis as:
> IMPROVING / UNCHANGED / DETERIORATING / BROKEN.
>
> Cite evidence for each material conclusion.

---

# 17. AI workflow — Portfolio review

## Prompt

> Review this portfolio table and identify:
> - concentration;
> - sector overlap;
> - correlated exposures;
> - positions with upcoming earnings;
> - positions whose thesis has not been updated recently;
> - positions whose valuation has moved materially.
>
> Do not recommend trades. Prioritize what I should review first and explain why.

---

# 18. AI workflow — Sell decision challenge

## Prompt

> I am considering selling this position. Use my original thesis and the latest information to evaluate whether the reason is:
> - thesis broken;
> - valuation;
> - portfolio concentration;
> - opportunity cost;
> - emotional reaction to price.
>
> Challenge my reasoning and identify what evidence would justify each conclusion.

---

# 19. AI workflow — Watchlist prioritization

## Prompt

> Rank this watchlist by research priority, not by expected return. Use:
> - magnitude of dislocation;
> - business quality;
> - valuation gap;
> - upcoming catalyst;
> - information freshness;
> - uncertainty.
>
> Explain which names deserve immediate research and which can wait.

---

# 20. Human decision

AI can:

- compress information;
- surface anomalies;
- compare thesis vs results;
- prioritize research.

The investor must still decide:

- whether the thesis is intact;
- whether expected return is attractive;
- whether risk is acceptable;
- whether position size is appropriate;
- whether to act.

---

# 21. Common mistakes

- checking prices constantly;
- confusing activity with discipline;
- selling winners automatically;
- averaging down without thesis review;
- keeping broken positions because of loss aversion;
- owning too many positions to monitor properly;
- over-diversifying into low-conviction ideas;
- using AI-generated rankings as trade instructions.

# 23. Final program integration

At this stage the student should have one end-to-end process:

**Learn → Understand → Value → Detect → Decide → Monitor**

AI accelerates:

**Read → Extract → Compare → Challenge → Update**

Human judgment controls:

**Buy → Hold → Add → Trim → Sell**

# 24. Graduation test

Apply the [Course 501 graduation rubric](../03_templates/templates_and_rubrics.md#501--manage-your-portfolio) and [canonical scoring threshold](../03_templates/templates_and_rubrics.md#5-universal-evaluation-rubric).

The student passes when they can manage the portfolio without needing to watch prices every day and can answer:

- Which positions require attention now?
- Which theses have changed?
- Where is the portfolio concentrated?
- What would make me sell?
- What opportunities deserve research?
- What can safely be ignored?

The final sign of maturity is not more activity.

It is knowing when no action is required.
